PORTFOLIO

Stanford Laptop Orchestra (SLOrk)

The laptop orchestra is a large-scale, computer-mediated ensemble that explores cutting-edge technology in combination with conventional musical contexts—while radically transforming both. This unique ensemble format comprises laptops, human performers, controllers, and custom multi-channel speaker arrays designed to provide each computer-based instrument with its own acoustic identity and presence. The orchestra fuses a powerful sea of sound with the immediacy of human music-making, capturing the irreplaceable energy of a live ensemble performance as well as its sonic intimacy. At the same time, the orchestra makes use of the computer’s capabilities to experiment with sounds, instruments, and new forms of musical expression. Offstage, the ensemble serves as a unique laboratory and classroom that explore music, computer science, interaction design, composition, and live performance. Since inception, ChucK has been a primary programming tool for all aspects of SLOrk (instrument design, sound synthesis, teaching, and more recently graphics and animation).

I have served as the founding Director of the Stanford Laptop Orchestra (SLOrk) since its inception in 2008. SLOrk (along with its predecessor and East Coast sibling, PLOrk) helped to "popularize" the medium within academia and with the mainstream press, and it is still going strong long since it was "all the rage". SLOrk continues because it is a unique place where people come to make music and make it together. "Innovation" in SLOrk involves technology but is ultimately never about the technology; rather, progress is measured by the continual creation of new pieces and performances and learning contexts. As the Director of the Stanford Laptop Orchestra, this has been my mission since bringing the idea from the Princeton Laptop Orchestra (PLOrk), founded by Dan Trueman and Perry Cook in 2005 at Princeton University (where I received my Ph.D. and helped to build and teach PLOrk). Since 2008, SLOrk has led to the creation and premiere of over 250 new works, including a feature-length "laptopera" (laptop orchestra opera). The amount of output in the forms of musical works, musical instruments, and performances would be too numerous to list. Instead, I have curated a selection of highlights below.

SLOrk's Salad Bowl Speakers

Laptop orchestras such as SLOrk represent an embodied inquiry into cutting-edge technology for music-making, without abandoning what we traditionally value about group-based music making—including physicality and acoustic presence. We built the SLOrk hemispherical speakers arrays out of wooden salad (from IKEA in 2008 and from Target in 2025). The hemispherical speaker arrays (see figures below) are designed to keep sound local to each instrument and human performer, an acoustic feature that profoundly influences how instrument and sound are designed, how music is written and performed, and how it is experienced by the audience.

Laptop Orchestra as Classroom

The Stanford Laptop Orchestra exists primarily in the form of an annual course: Music 128 / Computer Science 170 "Stanford Laptop Orchestra: Coding, Composition, and Performance". Students come from a diversity of disciplines ranging from Computer Science to Music to Human Biology to Philosophy to Graduate School of Education and more. By design, students engage in group work, but we make every effort to not designate specialties (e.g., we avoid having the computer science students do the coding; the music students do the composing, etc.). Instead, SLOrk fosters a "full-stack" mindset where each student engages broadly with each of the aspects of sound design, composition, instrument design, performance, and coding. While this intentional avoidance of specialty may seem less than "optimally efficient", we are insisting students step out of their comfort zones and learn to be accountable for all of what they do. We believe that this gives students broader perspectives and almost always leads to more authentic art. At Stanford, in the heart of Silicon Valley, where the pressure to be competitive/productive/impressive can be stifling, SLOrk is meant as a protected space to intensively explore technology and music as an ensemble, and where intention and craft is valued above achievement.

Towards the end of each season, SLOrk ensemble members participate in a three-day mini-residency at Bing Concert Hall to intensively prepare for the Spring Concert.

The SLOrk classroom in action: new instruments and new musical works are critiqued, workshopped, and rehearsed weekly.

Laptop Orchestra + Opera == Laptopera

The Stanford Laptop Orchestra (SLOrk) presents the first-ever laptopera. The Furies: A Laptopera is a retelling of the Greek tragedy Electra. Blending a number of versions of the Electra story including works by Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and Jean Paul Sartre, this retelling explores central questions regarding how communities escape from cycles of violence, the role of guilt and shame in community identity, personal responsibility, how justice interfaces with cycles of violence, and redemption. The artistic medium of the laptop orchestra both serves to recast the traditional instrumental role in a new kind of operatic medium (the “laptopera”) that reimagines the potential of instrument building to support dramatic elements and character relationships—while simultaneously positing questions about technology in our lives presently, both in its promise to help us flourish and in its perils to perpetuate and amplify the existing cycles of violence in our world today.

The laptopera was crafted specifically for the medium of the laptop orchestra, and featured custom instruments and live computer visuals. Pictured is The Rope, a shared physical instrument to be played by a group of performers, each of whom must account for their own (inter)actions as well as of those around them—embodying a fundamental duality of being an individual within a community.

The Furies: A Laptopera explores themes of guilt, shame, community, cycles of violence in a laptop orchestra-mediated retelling of Electra.

From inception to World Premiere. The Furies began as collaboration in 2017 between composer and PLOrk veteran Dr. Anne Hege and SLOrk to explore and create a laptop orchestra-based opera. Dr. Hege composed the music and wrote the libretto, while a team designed and built the instruments using ChucK and Wekinator. The production spanned five years and two different casts, due to the COVID-19 pandemic (which unfortunately further laid bare the many cycles of violence we perpetuate upon one another). COVID led to the final act of the opera being completely reconceived: away from redemption through divine intervention by the god Apollo, towards a humanistic contemplation on community and compassion that is far more open-ended in its interpretation. I co-produced the laptopera and also created the live visuals.

Images of SLOrk Past and Present

Over nearly two decades, the Stanford Laptop Orchestra has produced an expansive portfolio of musical works, new instruments, and performances. Each of these serve simultaneously as acts of engineering, artistic expression, and critical practice research into what it means to make computer music together as an ensemble and a community of musician tool-builders. We end this entry in the Portfolio with a few selected images of SLOrk from over the years.

2014: SLOrk in Beijing. A three week summer residency at the Stanford Center in Peking University, SLOrk presents a concert of new works.

2021: SLOrk members Xiaomian Yang and Tess Rinaldo rehearses their piece "Color of Glass", a duet that depicts two voices learning to harmonize with one another. They explore the extremes of their own sounds, and the boundaries between themselves and each other.

SLOrktastic Chamber Music 2017

2019: SLOrk performs at Bing Concert Hall. "Once & Done" by Hassan Estakhrian explores the sound of plastic as a sonic reflection on its use and impact on our world.

2023: There is a history of SLOrk works (and instruments) that blur the boundary between musical performance and dance.

Eating together is a valued tradition of SLOrk culture. I try to keep everyone happily fed through rehearsals and concerts. Each season culminates in an evening of hot pot.

SLOrk wouldn't work without the community and a sense of fun that serve as a cultural medium to do the work.

SLOrk has explored and incorporated a range of musical interactions: including physical gestures, live coding, audiovisual performance, dance, and virtual reality. "Aura" (2019) by Jack Atherton is an extended reality SLOrk performance where the conductor is in VR (and what he sees is projected on for the audience); all other players are physical-world entities with a one-to-one correspondence to entities in virtual reality.
